# Dossier 05 — Human Clinical Evidence and Human Pharmacology of *Scutellaria baicalensis*

Stream: clinical/human evidence (Brief 05). Prepared 2026-11-14. Every source was fetched and archived under `research/clinical/pages/`; structured quotes and claims are in `research/findings_clinical.json`.

## 1. Overview

Human evidence for *Scutellaria baicalensis* falls into four tiers. (i) A manufacturer-supported program for flavocoxid (Limbrel; sold as UP446/UP326): two naproxen-controlled knee osteoarthritis (OA) randomized trials (n = 103, 220), a placebo-controlled safety randomized controlled trial (RCT), a coagulation study, a 1067-patient open-label post-marketing study, and a 1-year phase 1/2 study in Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). (ii) Small modern RCTs of similar blends (influenza-vaccination immune response, joint-discomfort onset, cognition — the last negative) plus single-herb or constituent trials in type 2 diabetes (T2D), mood disorders, and periodontal health. (iii) A Chinese phase I program for purified baicalein (100–2800 mg) and, in 2026, purified oroxylin A tablets, defining human safety and pharmacokinetics (PK). (iv) Human PK/excretion studies since 1998 showing that what circulates is predominantly glucuronide and sulfate conjugates, not aglycone. No human study has tested a longevity endpoint. Human gut-microbiome data exist, including increased Akkermansia in T2D.

## 2. Human trials in depth

### 2.1 The flavocoxid osteoarthritis program

Flavocoxid is a proprietary mixture of baicalin and catechin flavonoid molecules from *S. baicalensis* root and *A. catechu* bark, marketed as the US prescription medical food Limbrel (Levy et al., Nutr Res 2009 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19555810/, accessed 2026-11-14).

- **Pilot RCT (2009):** 103 subjects with moderate knee OA, flavocoxid 500 mg twice daily vs naproxen 500 mg twice daily for 1 month; both arms improved (P ≤ .001) with no statistically detectable between-group differences, and a trend toward more edema and musculoskeletal discomfort with naproxen (Levy et al., Nutr Res 2009 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19555810/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Flagship RCT (2010):** 220 subjects with moderate-to-severe knee OA; randomized, multicenter, double-blind, noninferiority design; no efficacy difference vs naproxen in the intent-to-treat population, and significantly fewer upper gastrointestinal and renal (edema) adverse events with flavocoxid (Levy et al., Adv Ther 2010 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20845002/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Placebo-controlled safety RCT (2009):** 59 patients, 12 weeks, flavocoxid 250 mg twice daily; no differences in adverse events by body system, blood pressure, or any laboratory parameter (Morgan et al., J Med Food 2009 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19857081/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Coagulation study (2010):** flavocoxid did not inhibit thromboxane production, platelet aggregation, or bleeding times in healthy volunteers, and did not inhibit or potentiate the anticoagulant effect of warfarin in warfarinized OA patients (Pillai et al., Adv Ther 2010 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20556559/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **GOAL post-marketing study (2010):** 1067 patients at 41 US rheumatology practices, open-label, 60 days; physician global VAS improved from 60.1 to 42.5 by week 8, adverse-event incidence roughly 10%, gastroprotective medication use fell by more than 30%; the authors flag it as open-label and not rigorously controlled (Pillai et al., Curr Med Res Opin 2010 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20225990/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **DMD phase 1/2 (2021):** 17 boys took oral flavocoxid 250–500 mg for 1 year with no dropouts; IL-1β and TNF-α fell only in the steroid add-on subgroup and exploratory motor outcomes were not significant — the longest controlled flavocoxid exposure (Vita et al., Brain Sci 2021 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33467104/, accessed 2026-11-14).

**Quality characterization.** The OA trials are double-blind with active comparators, but no accessible abstract states allocation concealment or a Jadad-type score; several share manufacturer-affiliated authors (Levy, Pillai, Burnett); the flagship trial was powered for noninferiority vs naproxen, not placebo-superiority; GOAL was uncontrolled. Offsetting evidence: an independent placebo-controlled safety RCT found no laboratory signal, while the Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network (DILIN) prospectively captured 4 adults with acute hepatocellular injury beginning 1–3 months after starting flavocoxid (mean peak ALT 1268 U/L; all recovered after cessation) (Chalasani et al., Ann Intern Med 2012 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22711078/, accessed 2026-11-14). A US claims-data cohort (2006–2017; 3337 flavocoxid initiators propensity-matched 1:2 to 6674 NSAID users) found hospitalized liver-injury rates of 3.2 vs 2.4 per 1000 person-years (adjusted HR 1.16, 95% CI 0.23–6.01) and a significantly lower hospitalized GI-bleeding rate with flavocoxid (Curtis et al., Osteoarthritis Cartilage 2020 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32305527/, accessed 2026-11-14).

### 2.2 Other UP446/UP326 blend RCTs

- **Acute joint discomfort (2014):** 79 adults with mild-to-moderate knee OA, 1 week, UP446 500 mg/day vs naproxen 440 mg/day; significant time-dependent pain decrease with UP446 (P = .009) (Arjmandi et al., J Med Food 2014 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24611484/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Influenza vaccination (2023):** 50 healthy adults aged 40–80, triple-blind, 56 days of UP446 increased total IgA/IgG and influenza B-specific IgG after vaccination (Lewis et al., J Am Nutr Assoc 2023 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36413261/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Cognition (2025) — negative:** 85 healthy adults, 4 weeks of UP326 (240 mg *S. baicalensis* + 51 mg *A. catechu*); cognition improved over time in both arms with no between-group difference (Krieger et al., Nutr Neurosci 2025 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39701593/, accessed 2026-11-14).

### 2.3 Single-herb *S. baicalensis*, baicalin, and constituent trials

- **T2D add-on (2020):** crossover trial (8 weeks / 4-week washout / 8 weeks); patients on metformin added *S. baicalensis* 3.52 g/day; glucose tolerance improved and TNF-α expression fell vs placebo, gut Lactobacillus and Akkermansia increased remarkably, and some subjects developed high liver enzyme levels (Shin et al., Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab 2020 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31770016/, accessed 2026-11-14). The only located human trial showing *S. baicalensis*-driven microbiome reshaping, including Akkermansia.
- **Baicalin in coexisting coronary artery disease + rheumatoid arthritis (2018):** 374 patients, 12 weeks, baicalin 500 mg/day vs placebo improved triglycerides, total cholesterol, LDL, apolipoproteins, cardiotrophin-1, and hs-CRP, with good/moderate EULAR response in 71% vs 53% (Hang et al., Lipids Health Dis 2018 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29935544/, accessed 2026-11-14). Caveat: single-center, no visible registration, large unreplicated effects — weight cautiously.
- **Mood and stress (2025):** 180 adults with mild-to-moderate depressive symptoms took scutellaria extract, saffron, both, or placebo for 6 weeks; scutellaria showed a positive effect on mood regulation — the first human demonstration — with a synergistic combination effect (Dormal et al., Nutrients 2025 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40077679/, accessed 2026-11-14). Separately, 43 stressed adults in a 15-day crossover RCT of a *S. baicalensis*/*Crataegus* + magnesium/chromium supplement improved mood, trait anxiety, and working memory under stress (Dodd et al., J Psychopharmacol 2025 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41194549/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Periodontitis/gingivitis:** a 21-day experimental gingivitis study (n = 40) found a *S. baicalensis* toothpaste significantly reduced gingival index, plaque development, and vital flora vs placebo (Arweiler et al., Clin Oral Investig 2011 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20936314/, accessed 2026-11-14); a 3-month RCT in 110 orthodontic patients found a compound *S. baicalensis* gargle lowered plaque index, probing depth, and bleeding index and suppressed periodontal pathogens including *Porphyromonas gingivalis* (Zhang et al., Medicine 2024 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39183390/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Negative result (2009):** a 6-month double-blind trial in 85 children with asthma found no benefit of the five-herb CUF2 formula (including Radix scutellariae) over placebo (Wong et al., J Altern Complement Med 2009 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19821718/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Hepatic function monitoring (2019):** 17 patients took 1335 mg dried root daily for a mean 444 days with no significant change in ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, or bilirubin — uncontrolled pre-post, and the longest single-herb human exposure located (Puri et al., Med Hypotheses 2019 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31557595/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Ulcerative colitis:** only ex-vivo work exists — baicalin at 40 µmoL modulated STAT4/STAT6 phosphorylation in PBMCs from UC patients; no clinical trial located (Yu et al., Zhongguo Zhong Xi Yi Jie He Za Zhi 2015 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26043563/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- **Respiratory infection:** no single-herb efficacy RCT located; human baicalin PK comes from multi-herb Shuanghuanglian in healthy subjects (Zhang et al., J Sep Sci 2022 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35713475/, accessed 2026-11-14), and baicalein's phase I program was motivated by influenza but published no efficacy trial (Dong et al., J Ethnopharmacol 2021 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33753147/, accessed 2026-11-14).

## 3. Human pharmacokinetics

**Absorption is microbiota-dependent.** Native baicalin (baicalein 7-O-glucuronide) is poorly absorbed; enzymes of the colonic microbiota, β-glucuronidases, hydrolyze it to the aglycone baicalein, which is absorbed — in an in-vitro colon model (MimiCol), baicalin was converted to baicalein only when volunteer-derived microbiota were present (Seradj et al., Pharmazie 2024 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39152558/, accessed 2026-11-14). Human fecal flora convert *Scutellariae Radix* glucuronides to their aglycones (Zuo et al., Biol Pharm Bull 2002 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12033492/, accessed 2026-11-14), and baicalin/baicalein interconvert via phase II enzymes in the body (Noh et al., Molecules 2016 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26978333/, accessed 2026-11-14).

**What circulates.** In 14 healthy men after oral Sho-saiko-to, baicalein 6-O-sulfate was identified in plasma and plasma baicalin showed secondary peaks at 12 and 24 hours (Muto et al., Yakugaku Zasshi 1998 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9549431/, accessed 2026-11-14) — the double-peak signature of the enterohepatic recycling and gastrointestinal hydrolysis that characterize baicalin's PK profile (Huang et al., Eur J Drug Metab Pharmacokinet 2019 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30209794/, accessed 2026-11-14). After oral Shuanghuanglian, 13 baicalin-derived components were detected in human plasma, including parent baicalin (assay range 5–2000 ng/mL) and baicalein (1–100 ng/mL) (Zhang et al., J Sep Sci 2022 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35713475/, accessed 2026-11-14). Urinary recovery is minor: after 5.2 g *Scutellariae Radix* powder in 10 volunteers, baicalein conjugates totaled about 7.2% of dose, sulfates predominating (Lai et al., Biol Pharm Bull 2003 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12520178/, accessed 2026-11-14).

**Purified-constituent phase I (Chinese healthy volunteers).** Baicalein chewable tablets: single ascending doses 100–2800 mg in 72 adults were safe and well tolerated; median Tmax 0.75–3.5 h (baicalein) and 0.5–3 h (baicalin), urinary clearance <1%, ~27% eliminated unchanged in feces (Li et al., J Ethnopharmacol 2014 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25219601/, accessed 2026-11-14); multiple ascending doses 200–800 mg reached steady state by day 8 with accumulation indices 1.66–2.45 and no serious accumulation (Pang et al., Clin Drug Investig 2016 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27352310/, accessed 2026-11-14). A second tablet program showed rapid absorption (plasma peaks within 2 h) and urinary double peaks (Li et al., Clin Transl Sci 2021 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34156161/, accessed 2026-11-14); single doses 200–800 mg produced Cmax 280.44–845.20 ng/mL, t1/2 7.80–14.91 h, and less-than-dose-proportional exposure (Dong et al., J Ethnopharmacol 2021 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33753147/, accessed 2026-11-14). Oroxylin A tablets (2026): 400–2400 mg produced no dose-limiting toxicity, approximately dose-proportional Cmax, moderate accumulation (1.51–1.73-fold), and a 1.6-fold Cmax increase with a high-fat meal (Yang et al., Clin Transl Oncol 2026 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40903694/, accessed 2026-11-14).

**Interactions.** A single 500 mg baicalin dose did not clinically significantly alter cyclosporine A PK in 16 healthy Chinese volunteers (Liu et al., Front Pharmacol 2019 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31156436/, accessed 2026-11-14). In rats, *Scutellariae Radix* plus mefenamic acid showed minimal PK interaction but potentiated PGE2 inhibition and reduced NSAID gastric damage — animal evidence only (Fong et al., J Ethnopharmacol 2015 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25980420/, accessed 2026-11-14).

**Blood-brain barrier.** No human BBB penetration data were located. In mice, all six *Scutellariae Radix* flavones tested — aglycones baicalein, wogonin, oroxylin A plus glucuronides baicalin, wogonoside, oroxyloside — crossed the blood-brain barrier (7.9–224.0 pmol/g brain), oroxylin A highest, and the whole extract showed no significant anxiolytic effect at the studied doses (Fong et al., Mol Pharm 2017 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28426226/, accessed 2026-11-14).

**Bioavailability interventions — formulation research, not clinical evidence.** *S. baicalensis* flavonoids are limited by low water solubility, poor permeability, and microbial transformation; nanoformulations improve biopharmaceutical properties preclinically, but doubts remain about toxicity and real improvements in human absorption because of the limited number of clinical trials (Wang et al., Curr Top Med Chem 2023 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36443977/, accessed 2026-11-14). A 2026 ADME review concurs that nanodelivery remains preclinical and well-designed trials are still needed (Kumari & Kabra, Naunyn Schmiedebergs Arch Pharmacol 2026 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42105002/, accessed 2026-11-14). The only human-tested delivery manipulations are the chewable-vs-tablet baicalein programs and the food effect above.

**Gut microbiome interaction.** Human: Lactobacillus and Akkermansia increased remarkably in metformin-treated T2D patients receiving 3.52 g/day *S. baicalensis* (Shin et al., 2020 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31770016/, accessed 2026-11-14). Preclinical reviews describe baicalin as remodeling microbial community structure and metabolites, including short-chain fatty acid and bile-acid signaling, along gut-organ axes — while emphasizing that this evidence is preclinical (Zhu et al., Drug Des Devel Ther 2026 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41847306/, accessed 2026-11-14).

## 4. What is NOT established (gaps and failed hints)

1. **Direct longevity endpoints in humans: none.** No trial of *S. baicalensis*, baicalin, baicalein, or flavocoxid has measured mortality, lifespan, healthspan, or geroscience biomarkers. Longest human exposures located: 444 days dried root, uncontrolled (n = 17) (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31557595/); 1-year open-label flavocoxid in 17 DMD boys (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33467104/); 6-month negative multi-herb asthma trial (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19821718/). Controlled efficacy trials run ≤ 12 weeks.
2. **Flavocoxid RCT vs celecoxib — NOT FOUND.** Comparators were naproxen (PMIDs 19555810, 20845002) and placebo (PMID 19857081); PubMed "flavocoxid AND celecoxib" returns no clinical trial.
3. **"Korean Phase I of purified baicalein" — NOT FOUND.** All published phase I trials were conducted in Chinese healthy volunteers (PMIDs 25219601, 27352310, 34156161, 33753147).
4. **Jadad scores — not stated** in any accessible abstract of the flavocoxid trials; quality must be characterized by design features, not a published score.
5. **No single-herb RCTs located** for liver disease, respiratory infection, or metabolic syndrome; the diabetes evidence is a metformin-confounded add-on crossover (PMID 31770016); ulcerative colitis has only ex-vivo work (PMID 26043563).
6. **No human BBB penetration data** — rodent evidence only (PMID 28426226).
7. **Nanoformulation/phospholipid/SNEDDS work remains preclinical** — formulation research, not human evidence (PMIDs 36443977, 42105002).
8. **Replication risk:** the n = 374 baicalin CAD/RA trial (PMID 29935544) is unreplicated and lacks visible registration; several UP446 trials are industry-authored; the 2025 cognition trial was negative.

## 6. Open questions for the parent

1. **Attribution:** every skullcap OA efficacy RCT used the flavocoxid blend (*S. baicalensis* + *A. catechu*); present as baicalin-constituent evidence rather than single-herb *S. baicalensis* evidence?
2. **Weighting:** how much weight for the unreplicated n = 374 baicalin CAD/RA trial and the manufacturer-affiliated OA program vs the small independent 2020–2025 RCTs?
3. **Microbiome framing:** the human Akkermansia increase (Shin 2020) occurred with metformin co-therapy — present as combined-therapy signal, not standalone *S. baicalensis* effect.
4. **Liver safety:** DILIN cases and the claims-data study sit against the 444-day uncontrolled monitoring study; coordinate with the safety stream (Brief 06) — human data do not support a hepatoprotection claim.
5. **PK anchors if wanted:** baicalein Cmax 280–845 ng/mL at 200–800 mg, Tmax ≤ 3.5 h, t1/2 7.8–14.9 h, steady state day 8; circulating species are conjugates, minimal free aglycone (PMIDs 25219601, 33753147, 9549431).
