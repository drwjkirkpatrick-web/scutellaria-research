# Research dossier 03 — Longevity signaling pathways

**Scope:** baicalin, baicalein and wogonin actions on the canonical longevity signaling pathways. **Date:** 2026-11-14. All quotations are verbatim from saved page files (`research/longevity/pages/`); machine-readable record: `research/findings_longevity.json` (38 sources, 25 claims).

## 1 Overview

Preclinical evidence (2013–2026, weighted toward 2019–2026) documents actions of the three major *S. baicalensis* flavonoids on every assigned axis: sirtuins (SIRT1/SIRT3/SIRT6), AMPK, mTOR/autophagy, FOXO and insulin/IGF-1 signaling, Nrf2, NF-κB, mitochondrial quality control, and the CNS-aging enzymes MAO-B and BACE1. The evidence is almost entirely rodent and cell work; there is one direct lifespan experiment (*C. elegans*, baicalein) and one explicit caloric-restriction-mimetic (CRM) candidacy claim (baicalein, aged rats). No human trial measured a longevity-pathway endpoint. Baicalin is the best-characterized AMPK/insulin-signaling modulator; baicalein carries most of the autophagy, sirtuin and CNS-enzyme evidence; wogonin contributes MAO data. Caution: several effects are modulatory rather than uniformly activating (see (a), (b), §4).

## 2 Evidence in depth (by pathway)

### (a) Sirtuins

In high-fat-diet-fed and fasted mice, baicalin restrained excessive hepatic glucose production by decreasing NAD+-driven SirT1 induction and preserving STAT3 acetylation (Baicalin regulates SirT1/STAT3 pathway and restrains excessive hepatic glucose production. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30144531/, accessed 2026-11-14). In olfactory bulbectomized rats, baicalin (20 and 40 mg/kg) raised SIRT1, lowered acetylated p65, and cut hippocampal and hypothalamic IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α (Baicalin reverse depressive-like behaviors through regulation SIRT1-NF-kB signaling pathway in olfactory bulbectomized rats. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30848526/, accessed 2026-11-14). SIRT6: in MCAO/R mice and OGD/R-treated HT22 cells, baicalein upregulated SIRT6, deacetylating FOXA2 and promoting SLC7A11, limiting ferroptosis and apoptosis (Baicalein Inhibits Cerebral Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury through SIRT6-Mediated FOXA2 Deacetylation to Promote SLC7A11 Expression. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39299807/, accessed 2026-11-14); in a Scutellariae Radix–Coptidis Rhizoma NAFLD study, baicalin enhanced ACSL5 via SIRT6-mediated deacetylation, promoting fatty-acid oxidation (Scutellariae Radix and Coptidis Rhizoma improve NAFLD via regulation of SIRT6/ACSL5 pathway and SCD1. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40254111/, accessed 2026-11-14). SIRT3: baicalein (100 mg/kg/day orally) restored lung Sirt3 in bleomycin-treated mice, and Sirt3 siRNA abolished its anti-fibrosis, anti-senescence effect (Baicalein attenuates bleomycin-induced lung fibroblast senescence and lung fibrosis through restoration of Sirt3 expression. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36815239/, accessed 2026-11-14). A hepatic review lists baicalin among SIRT1-activating hepatoprotective flavonoids, with docking support (Flavonoids-mediated SIRT1 signaling activation in hepatic disorders. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32750437/, accessed 2026-11-14).

### (b) AMPK

A 2025 review documents baicalin regulation of AMPK and its upstream and downstream pathways across metabolic and inflammatory contexts (Regulatory Role of Flavonoid Baicalin from Scutellaria baicalensis on AMPK: A Review. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40374371/, accessed 2026-11-14). Anchors: baicalein activated AMPK and suppressed SREBP1 cleavage, lowering hepatic fat in HepG2 cells and high-fat-diet mice (Baicalein reduces hepatic fat accumulation by activating AMPK in oleic acid-induced HepG2 cells and high-fat diet-induced non-insulin-resistant mice. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31909773/, accessed 2026-11-14); baicalin (20 mg/kg gavage, 4 weeks) activated hippocampal AMPK/PGC-1α, with docking indicating direct AMPK binding (Baicalin ameliorates CUMS-induced depression-like behaviors through activating AMPK/PGC-1α pathway and enhancing NIX-mediated mitophagy in mice. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36463946/, accessed 2026-11-14); baicalein activated AMPK and inhibited mTOR at ≥5 µM in pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor lines, with in vivo xenograft activity (Baicalein activates 5' adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase, inhibits the mammalian target of rapamycin, and exhibits antiproliferative effects in pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors in vitro and in vivo. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36207198/, accessed 2026-11-14). Context note: in H2O2-stressed IPEC-J2 cells baicalin normalized stress-induced AMPK/Nrf2 activation, yet AMPK knockdown abrogated its protection (Baicalin Attenuates H2O2-Induced Oxidative Stress by Regulating the AMPK/Nrf2 Signaling Pathway in IPEC-J2 Cells. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37298392/, accessed 2026-11-14). No source names an upstream kinase such as LKB1 (see §4).

### (c) mTOR / autophagy

Autophagy dependence is proven by rescue experiments. Baicalein induced autophagic (not apoptotic) cell death via AMPK/ULK1 activation and mTORC1-component downregulation; Beclin-1/Vps34/Atg5/Atg7 knockdown completely reversed the death, and chloroquine enhanced LC3B flux (Baicalein induces autophagic cell death through AMPK/ULK1 activation and downregulation of mTORC1 complex components in human cancer cells. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25132405/, accessed 2026-11-14). Baicalin (50 and 100 mg/kg) inhibited Akt/mTOR/ULK1 to activate protective autophagy in bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis; 3-MA and hydroxychloroquine counteracted the anti-fibrotic effect (Baicalin inhibits the Akt/mTOR/ULK1 signaling pathway to activate autophagy and ameliorate pulmonary fibrosis. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41308360/, accessed 2026-11-14). In 6-OHDA-lesioned Parkinsonian rats, baicalein induced mitophagy via miR-30b and SIRT1/AMPK/mTOR, and 3-methyladenine blocked the neuroprotection (Baicalein Induces Mitochondrial Autophagy to Prevent Parkinson's Disease in Rats via miR-30b and the SIRT1/AMPK/mTOR Pathway. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35237220/, accessed 2026-11-14). Readouts across these studies: LC3-II conversion, p62 handling, Beclin-1 dependence, ULK1-axis inhibition.

### (d) FOXO / insulin-IGF-1 / PI3K-AKT

The only direct lifespan experiment: in *C. elegans*, baicalein extended lifespan and healthspan, suppressed DAF-2 insulin/IGF-1 signaling, promoted DAF-16 (FOXO) nuclear translocation and upregulated sod-3 (Baicalein Enhances Longevity and Healthspan of C. elegans Through the Insulin/IGF-1 Signaling Pathway. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41415716/, accessed 2026-11-14). Baicalein inhibited AKT to upregulate FOXO1 in glucocorticoid-treated MC3T3-E1 osteoblastic cells, raising ALP, OCN, Runx2 and Col-1 (Baicalein ameliorates osteoporosis via AKT/FOXO1 signaling. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34198266/, accessed 2026-11-14). Baicalin (50 mg/kg i.p., 21 days) raised pAKT, pAS160, GLUT4 and PGC-1α in obese-mouse skeletal muscle while lowering HOMA-IR and body weight (Baicalin against obesity and insulin resistance through activation of AKT/AS160/GLUT4 pathway. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28359800/, accessed 2026-11-14). Baicalein ameliorated insulin resistance via hepatic and muscular PI3K/AKT in a GLP-1R-dependent manner, lost in Glp1r-null mice (Baicalein Ameliorates Insulin Resistance of HFD/STZ Mice Through Activating PI3K/AKT Signal Pathway of Liver and Skeletal Muscle in a GLP-1R-Dependent Manner. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39456499/, accessed 2026-11-14).

### (e) Nrf2/ARE

Baicalin dose-dependently protected rat stomachs against stress-, ethanol- and indomethacin-induced ulcers (72% efficacy versus ranitidine, 80% against ethanol), attributed to Nrf2-mediated anti-secretory action (Baicalin protects against gastroduodenal ulcers via the modulation of Nrf2 expression: Experimental, biochemical, and histological analyses. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29128794/, accessed 2026-11-14). Baicalein activated Nrf2 in Hct116 cells with no change in Nrf2 ubiquitinylation, proteasomal activity or NRF2 transcription, and independently of redox modulation — not the canonical Keap1-degradation route (Modulation of the Nrf2 signalling pathway in Hct116 colon carcinoma cells by baicalein and its methylated derivative negletein. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27143122/, accessed 2026-11-14). A 2025 review documents baicalein modulation of the KEAP1/Nrf2 axis with HO-1, NQO1, GPx and SOD induction in neurodegeneration (Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects of baicalein: targeting Nrf2, and NFĸB in neurodegenerative disease. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40014253/, accessed 2026-11-14). AMPK/Nrf2 crosstalk is noted in (b). No epigenetic Nrf2 regulation was found (§4).

### (f) NF-κB inflammaging

Baicalin (12.5–50 µM) dose-dependently attenuated LPS-induced MyD88/NF-κB p65 activation and IL-6 release in RAW264.7 macrophages, and prevented LPS inflammation in mice by suppressing CD14 upstream of TLR4 (Baicalin prevents LPS-induced activation of TLR4/NF-κB p65 pathway and inflammation in mice via inhibiting the expression of CD14. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32457419/, accessed 2026-11-14). In hyperuricemic nephropathy mice and MSU-treated HK-2 cells, baicalin and baicalein docked to PI3K, AKT, p65 and IκBα, reduced p-PI3K, p-AKT and p-p65, and blunted TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-18 (Baicalin and baicalein attenuate hyperuricemic nephropathy via inhibiting PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signalling pathway. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36974463/, accessed 2026-11-14). Sirtuin cross-link: baicalin's SIRT1 effect reduced p65 acetylation in the depression model (Baicalin reverse depressive-like behaviors through regulation SIRT1-NF-kB signaling pathway in olfactory bulbectomized rats. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30848526/, accessed 2026-11-14). Review support for IĸB/NFĸB targeting by baicalein in neurodegeneration (Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects of baicalein: targeting Nrf2, and NFĸB in neurodegenerative disease. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40014253/, accessed 2026-11-14).

### (g) Mitochondrial quality control

Baicalin directly bound p38 MAPK, activating p38MAPK/PGC-1α and shifting skeletal-muscle fibers from glycolytic to oxidative in mice and piglets (C2C12: 125 µM) (Baicalin Promotes Skeletal Muscle Fiber Remodeling by Activating the p38MAPK/PGC-1α Signaling Pathway. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40103396/, accessed 2026-11-14). Baicalein restored mitochondrial function and biogenesis in rotenone-Parkinsonian rats (Therapeutic effects of baicalein on rotenone-induced Parkinson's disease through protecting mitochondrial function and biogenesis. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28855526/, accessed 2026-11-14) and enhanced NIX-mediated mitophagy in the chronically stressed hippocampus (Baicalin ameliorates CUMS-induced depression-like behaviors through activating AMPK/PGC-1α pathway and enhancing NIX-mediated mitophagy in mice. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36463946/, accessed 2026-11-14). In an amyloid-β-oligomer mouse model, baicalin reversed memory deficits and hippocampal mitochondrial fragmentation via PDE4/PKA/Drp1 (Baicalin attenuates amyloid β oligomers induced memory deficits and mitochondria fragmentation through regulation of PDE-PKA-Drp1 signalling. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35103832/, accessed 2026-11-14). SIRT3 restoration adds a mitochondrial-sirtuin quality-control mechanism (Baicalein attenuates bleomycin-induced lung fibroblast senescence and lung fibrosis through restoration of Sirt3 expression. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36815239/, accessed 2026-11-14).

### (h) Caloric-restriction-mimetic argument

In aged Wistar rats, baicalein improved the antioxidant profile and maintained redox homeostasis, "an effect that is similar to CR," and the authors concluded baicalein "may be a potent CRM candidate for antiaging interventions" (Baicalein May Act as a Caloric Restriction Mimetic Candidate to Improve the Antioxidant Profile in a Natural Rodent Model of Aging. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35316094/, accessed 2026-11-14). The co-occurrence of AMPK activation (b), SIRT1 modulation (a) and autophagy induction (c) is CRM-consistent, but no retrieved source classifies these constituents as CRMs on pathway-based criteria (§4).

### (i) CNS aging pharmacology: MAO-B, BACE1, amyloid

MAO-B potency: baicalin IC50 = 6.262 µM and baicalein IC50 = 21.01 µM against human recombinant MAO-B in vitro (prior report for baicalein: 22.1 µM); wogonin was weaker in that assay (79.24 µM) (Baicalin and baicalein from Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi alleviate aberrant neuronal suppression mediated by GABA from reactive astrocytes. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11076983/, accessed 2026-11-14). In LPS-inflamed mice, an ethanolic extract (300 mg/kg/day) and its compounds (baicalin 50, baicalein 5 mg/kg/day, i.p.) inhibited astrocytic MAO-B-driven GABA synthesis and reversed aberrant tonic inhibition (Baicalin and baicalein from Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi alleviate aberrant neuronal suppression mediated by GABA from reactive astrocytes. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38715318/, accessed 2026-11-14). Independently, wogonin competitively inhibited human MAO-A (IC50 6.35 µM, Ki 0.56 µM) and MAO-B (IC50 20.8 µM, Ki 1.96 µM), baicalein selectively inhibiting MAO-A; the 2024 authors discuss this inter-assay discrepancy (Potent inhibition of monoamine oxidase A by decursin from Angelica gigas Nakai and by wogonin from Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28109809/, accessed 2026-11-14; Baicalin and baicalein from Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi alleviate aberrant neuronal suppression mediated by GABA from reactive astrocytes. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11076983/, accessed 2026-11-14). In silico target fishing ranked MAO-B and COMT as the top two baicalein targets (In silico target fishing for the potential targets and molecular mechanisms of baicalein as an antiparkinsonian agent: discovery of the protective effects on NMDA receptor-mediated neurotoxicity. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23461900/, accessed 2026-11-14), and in MPTP-intoxicated mice baicalein raised tyrosine hydroxylase while decreasing MAO-B (Baicalein protects against MPP+/MPTP-induced neurotoxicity by ameliorating oxidative stress in SH-SY5Y cells and mouse model of Parkinson's disease. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34666128/, accessed 2026-11-14). BACE1: baicalein IC50 23.71 ± 1.91 µM (acetylcholinesterase IC50 45.95 ± 3.44 µM), docking-consistent (Baicalein as a Potential Inhibitor against BACE1 and AChE: Mechanistic Comprehension through In Vitro and Computational Approaches. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31703329/, accessed 2026-11-14). In amyloid models, baicalin rescued Aβ-oligomer-induced memory and mitochondrial deficits (Baicalin attenuates amyloid β oligomers induced memory deficits and mitochondria fragmentation through regulation of PDE-PKA-Drp1 signalling. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35103832/, accessed 2026-11-14); review evidence supports baicalein inhibition of disease-protein aggregation in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease (Therapeutic Potential of Baicalein in Alzheimer's Disease and Parkinson's Disease. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28634902/, accessed 2026-11-14). Baicalein and wogonin are reported to cross the blood-brain barrier (Baicalin and baicalein from Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi alleviate aberrant neuronal suppression mediated by GABA from reactive astrocytes. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11076983/, accessed 2026-11-14). No tau-model study was found (§4).

## 3 Human evidence

Three human datasets; none measured a longevity-pathway endpoint. (1) Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, n = 374 coronary artery disease and rheumatoid arthritis patients: baicalin 500 mg/day for 12 weeks significantly improved triglycerides, total and LDL-cholesterol, apolipoproteins, cardiotrophin-1 and hs-CRP versus placebo (71% vs 53% good/moderate EULAR response) (Baicalin reduces blood lipids and inflammation in patients with coronary artery disease and rheumatoid arthritis: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29935544/, accessed 2026-11-14). (2) Multiple-ascending-dose baicalein chewable tablets, 200–800 mg/day in healthy volunteers: safe and well tolerated, without serious accumulation (Parkinson's-disease development program) (Multiple-Ascending-Dose Pharmacokinetics and Safety Evaluation of Baicalein Chewable Tablets in Healthy Chinese Volunteers. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27352310/, accessed 2026-11-14). (3) Randomized crossover in type 2 diabetes patients on metformin: *S. baicalensis* 3.52 g/day for 8 weeks may improve glucose tolerance and inflammation, with Lactobacillus and Akkermansia expansion, though some subjects developed elevated liver enzymes (Combined effects of Scutellaria baicalensis with metformin on glucose tolerance of patients with type 2 diabetes via gut microbiota modulation. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31770016/, accessed 2026-11-14).

## 4 What is NOT established

- LKB1 or any other named upstream AMPK kinase as the mediator of constituent-driven AMPK activation: not identified in retrieved sources.
- Epigenetic regulation of Nrf2 (promoter methylation, histone marks) by these constituents: not found; the one mechanistic study shows ubiquitin-/proteasome-/transcription-independent, redox-independent activation (Modulation of the Nrf2 signalling pathway in Hct116 colon carcinoma cells by baicalein and its methylated derivative negletein. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27143122/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- Tau-dependent models: none retrieved; amyloid models only.
- CRM classification on pathway criteria: baicalein's CRM candidacy rests on antioxidant/redox endpoints, not AMPK/SIRT1 co-activation metrics (Baicalein May Act as a Caloric Restriction Mimetic Candidate to Improve the Antioxidant Profile in a Natural Rodent Model of Aging. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35316094/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- Direct IκBα-degradation readouts for a single constituent: IκBα appears only as a docking target (Baicalin and baicalein attenuate hyperuricemic nephropathy via inhibiting PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signalling pathway. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36974463/, accessed 2026-11-14); the one TLR4/IκBα record conflates baicalin and baicalein within its abstract and was not relied upon (Baicalin regulates TLR4/IκBα/NFκB signaling pathway to alleviate inflammation in Doxorubicin related cardiotoxicity. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36375245/, accessed 2026-11-14).
- The "baicalin rescuing SIRT1 in metabolic-disease models" hint is direction-dependent, not a general rescue: baicalin damps over-induced hepatic SIRT1 in fasting/high-fat-diet liver (Baicalin regulates SirT1/STAT3 pathway and restrains excessive hepatic glucose production. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30144531/, accessed 2026-11-14) and raises brain SIRT1 under neuroinflammatory stress (Baicalin reverse depressive-like behaviors through regulation SIRT1-NF-kB signaling pathway in olfactory bulbectomized rats. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30848526/, accessed 2026-11-14); no clean metabolic-disease SIRT1-rescue paper was identified.
- Human trials with longevity-pathway endpoints (AMPK/SIRT1/mTOR in human tissue): none exist.
